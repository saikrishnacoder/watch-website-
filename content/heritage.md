lede: The Heritage timeline — not the Heritage collection. Scroll the century from 1924 on the Rue du Rhône to the gold meridian written into the charter.

## 1924 — A maison in Geneva

HORLOGE opens on the Rue du Rhône. The first enamel dials carry a gold line at 12 — the Geneva meridian, drawn thinner than a hair.

## 1938 — Railroad minutes

Enamel dials settle into the proportion the Heritage line still keeps: a thin case, a chapter ring you can read at a glance, the meridian drawn last.

## 1947 — The club chronograph

Timing watches for Geneva motor clubs. The column wheel, the 10:10 pose, and a tachymeter that is still on Chronograph One.

## 1969 — We stayed mechanical

Quartz arrives. The atelier does not follow. Regulation in five positions becomes a rule, not a brochure line.

## 1984 — The watch returns

After-sales is written as a duty, not a department. A piece leaves Geneva once. It comes back for oil, regulation, and the occasional polite polish.

## 1998 — The meridian, named

What had been a finishing habit is written into the charter. Every dial, every line, one gold stroke at 12.

## 2018 — Chronograph One

The signature three-register. Ivory opaline, blued hands, a sapphire caseback. The maison’s most requested reference.

## 2024 — A century

One hundred years of composed time. Five lines share the seal. The meridian outlasts the first owner, as intended.

## 2026 — Meridian

The namesake line: GMT, worldtimer, dual time. HORLOGE is named for the clock. This line is named for the line.
